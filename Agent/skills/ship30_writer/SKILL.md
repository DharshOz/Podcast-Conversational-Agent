# Skill: Ship 30 for 30 Essay Writer

## When to use this skill
Use this skill when the user asks for an "essay," a blog-post-style writeup, or
explicitly requests "Ship 30 for 30 style" content -- as opposed to a normal
conversational Q&A answer. Do NOT use it for short factual questions.

## Output contract
- Format: Markdown essay
- Length: ~1250 words (1150-1350 acceptable)
- Must include: a strong hook, heavy use of bold text and bullet points for
  skimmability, and a clear closing takeaway.

## Style rules (apply all of these)

1. **Hook first, no throat-clearing.** Open with 1-2 sentences that immediately
   create curiosity or tension -- state a surprising claim, a sharp contrast, or
   a specific promise. Never open with generic framing like "In this essay, we
   will discuss..."

2. **Headline/title must contain, implicitly or explicitly:** what this is about,
   who it's for (if the topic is niche), how the reader should feel, and the
   payoff of reading. Prefer clarity over cleverness -- a title that is
   unambiguous about its payoff outperforms a witty, vague one.

3. **Wheels and spokes structure.** Organize the body into 3-5 main sections,
   each under one consistent organizing pattern -- pick exactly ONE of:
   Steps, Lessons, Mistakes, Reasons, Truths. Do not mix patterns within one
   essay (e.g. don't have "Step 1" then "Lesson 2").

4. **Bold key phrases throughout**, especially:
   - The first few words of a paragraph that state its core claim
   - Numbers, outcomes, and specific claims
   - Do not bold entire sentences -- bold the 3-8 word phrase that carries
     the point.

5. **Convert any "listing" sentence into a bulleted list.** If a paragraph
   would otherwise say "there are three things you need: X, Y, and Z," break
   it into a bullet list instead.

6. **Vary sentence rhythm.** Alternate short, punchy sentences (5-10 words)
   with longer explanatory ones. Avoid strings of same-length sentences.
   A good local pattern: short opener -> 2-3 sentences of build -> short
   closer, repeated across paragraphs.

7. **Fast rate of revelation.** Every sentence must add new information.
   Never restate the previous sentence in different words. Cut any sentence
   that doesn't move the point forward.

8. **Ground advice in something concrete** -- a specific example, scenario, or
   detail pulled from the source material -- rather than leaving advice fully
   abstract. Actionable claims paired with a concrete anchor are more
   memorable than either alone.

9. **Closing takeaway.** End with a short, compressed paragraph (3-5 sentences)
   that restates the core payoff in the most distilled form possible --
   written so it could stand alone as a summary if someone only read the end.

## Source material handling
Ground the essay's substance in the retrieved transcript material provided to
you. Do not fabricate quotes or attribute claims to the guest that don't
appear in the retrieved content. If the retrieved material is thin, say so
briefly rather than padding with generic filler.
